💡 Expressions to Refine (다듬으면 좋을 표현)

"I cannot eat a beef": This is a very common point of confusion for learners. For meat, produce, and other uncountable nouns, we generally don't use "a" or "an".
Native Alternative: "I can't eat beef." or "I don't eat beef."

"I like a man who is...": When talking about general preferences for a type of person, it's more natural to use the plural form.
Native Alternative: "I like men who are..." or "I like guys who are..."

  • Clear Pronunciation and Delivery: Her voice is clear and confident, making it easy for listeners to follow along. This is a huge asset in conversation.
  • Proactive Conversational Skills: She actively engages others by asking questions like "Have you taken the TOEIC exam?" and "What's your favorite character?", which keeps the conversation flowing and interesting.

📚 Personalized English Tips (개인별 영어 학습 팁)

Sera already has a strong foundation. To reach the next level, the focus should be on adding sophistication and naturalness to her already accurate English.

  • Advanced Shadowing: When listening to English news or podcasts, pay close attention to the small words: articles (a/the), prepositions (in/on/at), and adverbs (even/just). Mimic not just the content, but the rhythm and use of these "connector" words.
  • Paraphrasing Practice: After making a sentence, ask yourself, "How else could I say this?" For example, take "I like kind men" and try rephrasing it as "I'm attracted to men with a kind personality" or "Kindness is a quality I look for in a man." This expands both vocabulary and sentence flexibility.

📚 Personalized English Tips (개인별 영어 학습 팁)

Sony's main goal should be building confidence. Focusing on a few high-impact grammar points will make his English sound much more natural, which will in turn boost his confidence.

  • Master the Golden Rule: -ing vs. -ed: This is crucial. Things (a movie, a place) are **boring** or **interesting**. People feel **bored** or **interested**. (e.g., "Okinawa was a bit boring, so I felt bored.")

💡 Expressions to Refine (다듬으면 좋을 표현)

"I haven't never participated...": A common slip-up called a double negative. In standard English, you only need one negative word.
Native Alternative: "I have **never** participated..." or "I **haven't ever** participated..."

"Just a normal, obvious story": While "obvious" works, other words are more common for describing predictable plots.
Native Alternative: "It's just a typical, **cliché** story." or "It's a pretty **predictable** story."

📚 Personalized English Tips (개인별 영어 학습 팁)

Luke is past the "learning" stage and is now in the "refining" stage. The key to reaching the next level is to focus on the small details that separate a fluent speaker from a native-sounding one.

  • Self-Correction Through Recording: Record yourself speaking for a few minutes. When you listen back, you'll likely catch small, "fossilized" errors yourself, like saying "doesn't" instead of "don't". This is a powerful way to eliminate those minor mistakes.
  • Pay Attention to Prepositions: Focus on common pairings. For example, it's "rude **to** someone," not "rude for someone." Learning these as chunks (e.g., "interested **in**," "good **at**") is highly effective.

💡 Expressions to Refine (다듬으면 좋을 표현)

"I, I drunk a lot...": This is one of the most common verb mistakes in English! The simple past tense of "drink" is "drank".
Corrected: "I **drank** a lot..."

"My work needs me using the English a lot": The structure is a bit unnatural. There are simpler, more direct ways to say this.
Native Alternative: "My work **requires me to use** English a lot." or, more simply, "I **need to use** English a lot for my work."

💡 Expressions to Refine (다듬으면 좋을 표현)

"I my job is IT engineer": This sentence has two subjects ("I" and "my job"). The key is to choose one and build the sentence from there.
Corrected: "**I am** an IT engineer." or "**My job is** an IT engineer."

"I running at Han river": To talk about a regular habit, we use the simple present tense.
Corrected: "I **run** at the Han River."
